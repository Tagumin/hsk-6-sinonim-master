# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Indonesian HSK 6 learners sitting down to disambiguate Mandarin near-synonyms and retain them. Typical session: evening study at a desk, comparing two or three words that already look familiar, then locking the distinction with quiz or spaced repetition. They already know a lot of vocabulary; the job is nuance, collocation, and not mixing pairs like 突然 / 忽然.

## Product Purpose

A focused synonym-discrimination trainer for HSK 6: browse by category, contrast words in a group, practice collocations, review with Leitner, and see mastery progress. Success is a learner leaving a session knowing *when* to use one word versus another, with progress that survives refresh. The tool should disappear into the study task.

## Brand Personality

Quiet, scholarly, precise. A study desk, not a game. Voice is Indonesian for chrome and English/Chinese for content, calm and specific. Emotional goal: focused confidence after comparing a pair, not excitement.

## Anti-references

- Generic AI SaaS: dark slate, sky glow, bento cards, neon progress pills, glass header
- Gamified language apps: streaks-as-identity, mascots, loud celebration, XP theater
- Flashy dictionary UIs: rainbow tags, badge pile-ups, competing accents on every card

## Design Principles

- Distinction first: every screen should make the *difference between words* easier to see than the chrome around them.
- One study move at a time: browse, contrast, quiz, or review; do not compete for the same attention.
- Desk, not dashboard: density and type do the work; glow, pills, and hero metrics do not.
- Trust through familiarity: standard nav, search, and progress patterns; no invented affordances.
- Progress is quiet: mastery is a record, not a celebration.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA. Readable type, visible focus, keyboard for search, quiz, and navigation. Do not rely on color alone for mastered vs unmarked. Hanzi must remain large enough to read; pinyin must stay contrast-safe against the surface.
